The calendar the cadences are counted in

Every cadence here — daily, weekly, monthly — is counted in the 360° symmetrical calendar: twelve months of thirty days, five six-day weeks to a month, and a day lengthened so that 360 of them span one solar revolution exactly.

The arithmetic is why it is used. Every month is the same length and begins on the same weekday, so a monthly reset always falls the same distance from the weekly one and the two never drift apart. On the Gregorian grid a “monthly” cadence slides between 28 and 31 days and silently decouples from anything weekly; here the intervals nest — 6, 30, 360 — which is what makes a protocol built on phase relation schedulable at all.

The day itself is a true rotation of ordinary length — the calendar puts its remainder in five days outside the count rather than in the length of the day — so anything entrained to light keeps its real hour. The 18-hour window, the bedtime reset and the sleep architecture the third phase depends on sit on the solar day, and the 360° count governs the intervals between them.

3.1 Active TET1–3 demethylation & TDG base excision

Enzymatic erasure

Active reversal of age-associated hypermethylation drift is driven by Ten-Eleven Translocation (TET1, TET2, TET3) dioxygenases. Utilizing intracellular $\alpha$-ketoglutarate ($\alpha$-KG), $O_2$, and $Fe^{2+}$ cofactors, TET enzymes iteratively oxidize 5-methylcytosine ($5\text{mC}$):

$$5\text{mC} \xrightarrow{\text{TET}} 5\text{hmC} \xrightarrow{\text{TET}} 5\text{fC} \xrightarrow{\text{TET}} 5\text{caC}$$

Thymine DNA Glycosylase (TDG) cleaves the $N$-glycosidic bond of $5\text{fC}$ and $5\text{caC}$, generating an apurinic/apyrimidinic (AP) abasic site. APE1 / Ref-1 then hydrolyzes the phosphodiester backbone $5'$ to the lesion, creating a single-strand break with a $3'\text{-OH}$ primer terminus and a blocking $5'\text{-dRP}$ flap.

In plain terms — the rust eraser

Think of DNA as a master cookbook. As cells get older, sticky chemical rust spots (methyl marks) gum up the recipe pages so the cell can’t read them. TET enzymes act like a rust converter, turning the hard rust into brittle chalk. Then TDG acts like a hole punch, popping the rusted letter clean out of the page so it is ready for a brand-new letter.

3.2 Physical gap resynthesis via Polymerase-$\beta$

BER gap-filling

DNA Polymerase $\beta$ ($\text{Pol }\beta$) executes Base Excision Repair (BER) gap filling via a bifunctional, dual-domain catalytic architecture:

  • $8\text{ kDa}$ amino lyase. Excises the blocking $5'\text{-dRP}$ residue via $\beta$-elimination to expose a clean $5'\text{-phosphate}$.
  • $31\text{ kDa}$ polymerase domain. Coordinates dual $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions to insert an unmodified deoxycytidine monophosphate (dCTP) opposite the template guanine:
$$\text{Gap}_{(3'\text{-OH} / 5'\text{-P})} + \text{dCTP} \xrightarrow{\text{Pol }\beta, \text{Mg}^{2+}} \text{DNA}_{\text{inserted}} + \text{PP}_i$$

The DNA Ligase III / XRCC1 complex seals the phosphodiester backbone, permanently restoring an unmethylated, youthful cytosine base.

In plain terms — the precision typewriter

After the rusted letter is popped out, the cell needs to replace it. Pol β is a tiny two-handed typewriter: its left hand snips away the torn paper edge, and its right hand stamps in a fresh, unblemished letter ‘C’ (dCTP). Molecular glue (DNA ligase) then seals the paper smooth.

3.3 Polymerase thiol redox preservation (DHLA axis)

Thiol redox
