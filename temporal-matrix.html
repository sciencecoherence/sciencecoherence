Reading the instrument

Four clocks, one rhythm.

The dial is read from the outside in: the astral year, the orbital year, the day, and the descent. The orbital year counts the revolution itself, one date for each degree, 360 dates in twelve months of thirty. The astral year is exactly as long as one full descent, τ* Matrix dates, and it is divided by the descent's own spans. Each year is born from the sky: at its Turning the mean tropical year of that instant is taken, and the year's length, its time waves and its descent all follow from it. Every year the entropic energy is released again from J₀ to zero, and the instant it reaches zero the next year begins.

Where it comes from

One date, one degree Chosen: the founding decision

Everything in the instrument follows from one decision: a Matrix date is one degree of the Earth's revolution around the sun. The tropical year, the time from one March equinox to the next, is read from the sky at the opening of each year. For the year on the dial, Cycle 1, it is the mean tropical year at its Turning, Y = 365.2421896698 days; divided into 360 equal parts, each part lasts

Y / 360 = 1.0145616380 days = 24 h 20 m 58.126 s

This is the length of every Matrix date. It is one degree of the mean revolution. The real orbit is an ellipse, faster near the January perihelion, so counted from the March equinox the Earth's true position falls behind the counted degree by up to about four degrees by early autumn and meets it again at the next equinox. The clock shows both: ☉ the sun's true longitude, and the degree counted in Matrix dates since the equinox.

The dilation Derived: D₀

A Matrix date is longer than a civil day by D₀ = 20 m 58.126 s. The clock carries this difference inside the night: it races from 00:00 to 04:00, gaining the whole D₀ while the body sleeps, and repays it by noon. Noon therefore always falls on peak sun and the afternoon runs true; the extra length never displaces the light the body entrains to.

α, J0 and Q Derived: α, J0, Q

The same difference, as a fraction of a day, is α:

α = Y / 360 − 1 = 5.2421896698 / 360 = 0.014561637971… ≈ 1 / 68.67

Because Y is given to ten decimal places, α is exactly a ratio of whole numbers. Written out and reduced by 6, the largest factor its two terms share, it is

α = 52 421 896 698 / 3 600 000 000 000 = 8 736 982 783 / 600 000 000 000

The fraction cannot be reduced further. Its numerator is the entropic energy of the year, J0 = 8 736 982 783, and its denominator the full recurrence depth, Q = 600 000 000 000. Neither is chosen; both are α itself, written in its lowest terms, so that α = J0 / Q. The sky's year shortens a little every year, so each year has its own J0 and its own Q.

The descent, the time waves, the year Derived

α is expanded as a continued fraction:

α = [0; 68, 1, 2, 15, 1, 2, 2, 3, 19, 1, 1, 22, 1, 1, 10, 2, 8, 1, 19]

Its successive convergents are the rungs of the descent, the states the year passes through as J falls from J0 to zero. Its 19 partial quotients are the year's 19 time waves, each time wave carrying one as its number. Summing the time the energy is held at each rung, dτ/dq = J / J0 from q = 0 to Q, gives the length of the year, τ* ≈ 715.5313 Matrix dates. Nothing else is introduced: from one date, one degree, come the day, the dilation, J0, the ladder, the time waves and the year.

The body's own day Observed Framework prediction

Kept away from daylight and every clock, the human circadian system does not run on 24 hours. Under the controlled dim-light protocols of the last decades its intrinsic period averages about 24.2 h: 24.18 h in Czeisler and colleagues' 1999 study in Science, and 24.15 ± 0.2 h across 157 people in Duffy and colleagues' 2011 study in PNAS, with women slightly shorter than men. The earliest free-running studies, in which people chose their own light, found periods closer to 25 h. The measured period depends on the conditions of measurement.

The framework predicts that the body's native day is the Matrix date, 24 h 20 m 58 s, about 24.35 h, and that the lower averages reflect the conditions under which it has been measured. This is a prediction, not an established result: 24.35 h lies within the spread of individual periods, about one standard deviation above today's average. Living by a clock of that length, anchored to the sun, is what the protocol and its daily journal are built to test.

The orbital year One date, one degree

The ring between the astral year and the day is the orbital calendar. It counts the same Matrix dates as the astral year, continuously from the launch at the March equinox, and every 360 of them it completes one revolution: twelve months of thirty dates, each month thirty degrees of the orbit and exactly five weeks of six. Its months are named by the geometry of the orbit rather than by the seasons of either hemisphere: the sun's two crossings of the equator, its northern and southern turns, and the Earth's farthest and nearest points from the sun. On the ring the lime point is the counted degree and the amber ☉ is the sun's true longitude; the two separate by up to about four degrees through the year and meet again at the equinox. A year of the Temporal Matrix lasts τ* Matrix dates, and τ* is different every year. For Cycle 1 it is τ* = 6 251 584 658 434 / 8 736 982 783 ≈ 715.5313 Matrix dates, about 725.95 civil days. The time waves Intervals derived Names authored

The year's divisions are not chosen. They are the spans between the hard rungs of the descent, and their lengths come out of the continued fraction of α = J₀/Q. Each time wave carries a number, the partial quotient of that continued fraction which produces the rung closing it. This year there are 19: 68, 1, 2, 15, 1, ….

The time waves are named by their place in the year. Every year opens with Origo, Limen, Scissa, Vortex and Sedes and runs on through Nexus, Fornax, Lyra, Echo, Nodus, Corpus, Spira, Acus, Filum, Petra, Lumen and Conflux as far as it reaches; a longer year continues with Vela, Speculum, Clavis, Pons, Vena, Gemma, Arcus, Stella, Silentium, Sal, Aura, Limbus and Quies. Every year closes with Una and Monas, and that is not a choice: the last rung before zero always holds exactly one unit of energy, J = 1, so the last time wave is always the Last Unit. A place keeps its meaning from year to year; its number, its length and its dates are the sky's. For a century and more around the launch every year opens with the same numbers, 68, 1, 2, 15, 1, and over many centuries only 68, 1, 2 hold. A time wave shorter than seven dates is a gate, a passage rather than a season; this year has 5. A gate can even be shorter than a single date and open and close inside it. The time waves fall into five tides by how much energy is still held, and together they make an astrology of the Matrix itself: a map of where each year's energy is released, and of what each stretch of time asks for.     What is declared Framework definitions

    That each year is born from the sky: its Y is the mean tropical year at its Turning, from Laskar's formula, rounded to ten decimals, and everything in that year is derived from it. That one year is exactly one descent, τ* Matrix dates, and that the descent begins again at every turning. That the Matrix date keeps its full dilation for ever, with noon on peak sun.

    What does not line up Open

    Because each year's τ* is born from a slightly different Y, year lengths are not regular: the first cycles run between about 650 and 850 Matrix dates, from under two to well over two tropical years. The turning therefore wanders through the seasons without a fixed rhythm. It is shown, not adjusted: each year's length is derived, and bending it to the seasons would be fitting a constant to close a pattern.

    Declared constants

    Each year's Y comes from Laskar's formula for the mean tropical year, Y(T) = 365.2421896698 − 6.15359·10−6 T − 7.29·10−10 T2 + 2.64·10−10 T3 days, with T in Julian centuries from J2000.0, evaluated at the instant of the year's Turning and rounded to ten decimals. The real mean year shortens by about 0.53 s per century, which moves the tenth decimal about every fourteen hours, and beyond the first five numbers that is enough to give each year entirely different time waves. The founding value, 365.2421896698, is the mean year at J2000.0, 1 January 2000 at 12:00; it no longer runs any year, but its canon table is checked each time the page loads, to prove the exact arithmetic every year relies on.
